How Block0 works

Every score here is reconstructed from public Robinhood-Chain transfer data and is reproducible — no black box, no proprietary feed. This page is the whole method, and an honest measurement of how well it actually separates winners from the rest.

Signal, not proof — never a buy recommendation. Block0 is a forensic and pattern tool: it tells you whether a launch is clean and whether it looks like past winners looked. That is a position read, not a prediction, and certainly not financial advice.

The risk verdict

The headline 0–100 risk score is a transparent weighted composite of five sub-scores, each computed from the token's own transfer history over its real distributed float (the bonding curve and known venues are excluded so their reserves don't read as a giant holder).

Sub-scoreWhat it measuresWeight*
ConcentrationShare of supply held by the top 10 wallets, above a healthy baseline30–42%
BundlesCoordinated same-block buying at launch (one actor, many wallets)10–24%
Dumping nowInsiders (snipers + bundles) net-selling in the live 30-min window22–28%
SnipersWallets that bought within 3 blocks of the first pool trade, and their held share12–16%
DeployerShare of supply still sitting in the deployer's wallet8%

*Weights shift once a token graduates: concentration and live dumping matter more for an established coin, launch-mechanics (bundles) matter less. On top of the weighted mean sit two disqualifying floors — near-total concentration (top-10 ≥ 82–92%) or two-plus insiders selling right now force a high score regardless of everything else, because those single-handedly define the risk.

Blueprint fit — the winner fingerprint

Separately from risk, blueprint fit (0–100) asks how well a fresh launch matches the shape the biggest past graduations had. Validated discriminators, in order of weight:

  • No bundles (up to 40 pts) — the hard veto; coordinated launches rarely become winners.
  • A float that opens up (up to 25 pts) — top-10 concentration falling below 40–60%.
  • A settled, clean score (up to 20 pts) — risk under 25–45.
  • Real adoption (up to 15 pts) — 80–200+ actual holders.

It deliberately does not reward low sniper activity — the winner cohort showed heavy early snipers were survivable, while bundles were what killed. A strong fit is worth watching; it is a pattern, not a promise (see the measured limits below).

The winner corridor & valuation ladder

These come from a study of the top graduated cohort: each winner's whole history is replayed and reduced to a trajectory (distribution health blended with real wallet inflow) and a wallets→market-cap curve.

  • The corridor is the winners' healthy trajectory zone (25th–75th percentile) at each age. A live token's own trajectory is drawn through it — inside the band is tracking the winners, below is drifting.
  • Stage targets — each corridor box also shows the median unique-wallet count and market cap the winners actually carried by that age, so you see whether a token is hitting the real adoption and valuation a main player needs at each stage.
  • The valuation ladder places a token's current wallet count against what winners were worth at the same count.

The model is a committed, reproducible pipeline — the cohort backtests build the study data, which is assembled into the live model. It refreshes as more tokens graduate.

Limits — what this can't tell you

  • Prices are reconstructed. There's no oracle; price is implied from swap receipts and smoothed. Treat exact figures as estimates, not quotes.
  • On-chain can't see intent. A clean distribution can still be a slow rug; a messy one can still run. This reads structure, not the future.
  • The cohort is small and survivorship-shaped. Winners are defined by having already graduated. The signal is suggestive, not deterministic.
  • Fresh is uncertain. As measured above, the first half hour barely discriminates. Earliest is riskiest — that's inherent, not a bug.
